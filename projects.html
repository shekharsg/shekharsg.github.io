Project 01

District-scale nitrogen surplus in Indian agriculture

This work addresses a major gap in long-term subnational nitrogen information by constructing annual district-level agricultural nitrogen budgets from 1966 to 2017. Multiple estimates are used to propagate uncertainty and provide a robust base for spatial aggregation and policy-oriented assessment.

Objectives

  • Estimate annual district nitrogen surplus over five decades.
  • Represent methodological uncertainty using twelve alternative budget estimates.
  • Enable transparent spatial and temporal analysis of nitrogen-use performance.

Project 02

Indian interstate trade & nutrient pollution

India’s food system depends on large interstate movements of cereal crops. This project traces how nitrogen and phosphorus surpluses are embedded in those flows, identifying where trade shifts environmental burdens toward major production hubs and water-stressed regions.

Key finding

Production hubs account for roughly half of India’s trade-transferred nutrient surplus in the original analysis, illustrating how food security and regional pollution can become tightly coupled through trade.

Project 03

Crop restructuring for lower environmental costs

This study moves from diagnosis to intervention: it tests how agricultural production can be spatially restructured to reduce nitrogen pollution, water use and greenhouse-gas emissions while maintaining food production and reshaping trade.

Published outcome

The nitrogen-focused restructuring strategy reduces nitrogen surplus and water use while maintaining baseline calories, with wider co-benefits for greenhouse-gas emissions and socio-environmental costs.
